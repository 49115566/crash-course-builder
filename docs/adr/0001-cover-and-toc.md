# ADR 0001: Cover page and table of contents in Phase 1

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-10-21

## Context

The product asks for a book-like cover page and a table of contents. Asking the model to generate a
table of contents alongside numbered sections creates two overlapping lists that can disagree with
each other. It also makes outline parsing less reliable and encourages duplicated section titles or
link lists in the generated Markdown.

The reader needs a reliable, interactive table of contents regardless of what the model returns.

## Decision

Prompt v1 asks the model for:

1. Exactly one H1 course title.
2. A short cover block with promise, audience, and summary.
3. Numbered H2 section headings in the form `## n. Section title`, each with 2–5 bullets.
4. No separate table of contents or link list in the model output.

`outline.md` is generated by the application as a human-readable cover plus numbered outline. The
reader's "In this course" card and contents sidebar are the application's table of contents,
derived from the canonical course headings. Section headings are normalized to `## n. Title` at
assembly time so their anchors are stable.

The parser still tolerates a model that ignores the instruction: a heading named "Table of
Contents", "Contents", "Course Outline", or "Outline" and its link list are ignored.

## Consequences

- The outline parser has one canonical section source: H2 headings or a numbered list fallback.
- The reader controls TOC behavior, linking, scrollspy, and mobile presentation without trusting
  generated HTML.
- `course.md` contains the title, cover, and expanded sections; it never embeds the outline.
- `outline.md` remains useful on disk and can be displayed independently.
- Any future change to this interpretation is a prompt/version change and requires updated snapshots
  and documentation.
